Refresh Priority Scoring: Predicting At-Risk Content from Search Performance Signals

Refresh Priority Scoring: Predicting At-Risk Content from Search Performance Signals

Ayesha Riaz

FlyRank ML Internship · 2026

A transparent, client-held-out machine-learning approach for prioritizing content review and refresh decisions.

Abstract

Content teams managing large sites can't manually review every page to decide what needs refreshing, so this study asks: can search performance signals predict which pages are at risk of traffic decline well enough to prioritize a refresh queue? Using 90-day rolling windows of impressions, click, position, and query-diversity signals from the FlyRank search warehouse, I trained a Random Forest classifier to predict whether a page's impressions would decline more than 20% month-over-month, validating on entirely held-out clients to test whether the pattern generalizes rather than memorizes. The model reached 84.9% accuracy on unseen clients, only 1.5 points below its random-split accuracy, indicating the signal is genuinely transferable rather than client-specific. This substantially outperformed a rule-based baseline (46.5% accuracy) built from a comparable set of warehouse features. The result is a ranked, reason-coded refresh queue that gives content teams a defensible starting point for prioritization — not a guarantee of what will happen to any single page.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Large sites accumulate more content than any team can manually audit on a regular cadence. The practical question isn't "what's wrong with this page" for every page — it's which pages to look at first.

This paper frames that as two linked decisions: (1) is this page currently at risk of a meaningful traffic decline, and (2) given that, what refresh action does it warrant. A model that can rank pages by risk, with an explanation attached, turns an unbounded manual review problem into a bounded weekly queue.

2. Data

This analysis uses the FlyRank Internship Warehouse release, accessed via the gated Hugging Face dataset, covering the date range available in the release. Features were built directly from fact_content_daily and fact_content_query_90d using DuckDB aggregation over the full approximately 79M-row release, rather than the pre-built lane sample.

Content items with fewer than 150 impressions in the prior 90-day window were excluded to avoid scoring pages with too little history to produce a stable signal. No client names, domains, URLs, or raw query text are included in this paper or its outputs — all identifiers are anonymized hashes as provided by the release.

3. Methodology

Label definition

A page is labeled declining if its impressions in the most recent 90-day window are more than 20% lower than the prior 90-day window. This is a relative, within-page comparison, not an absolute traffic threshold.

Features

All features are computed from the prior 90-day window only, strictly before the window used to compute the label, to prevent leakage: prior-window impression volume (imp_prev90), rare-query impression share, visible-query count, anonymized-impression share, top-query concentration share, and weekend click share.

Validation design

Two splits were compared: a standard random 75/25 split, and a GroupShuffleSplit on client_hash_id that holds out entire clients rather than individual pages. The group split is the more honest test of generalization, since a model that only works within clients it has already seen would be far less useful in production, where new clients have no training history.

Baseline

A rule-based score from an earlier assignment was reconstructed here using only warehouse-available features (prior impression volume and average position), thresholded at the bottom 35th percentile as "at risk" to roughly match the model's positive rate. This is a simplified version of the original rule — full CTR, engagement, and content-age signals from the original baseline were not available in this feature table, which is treated as a limitation rather than hidden.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

This model is decision-support, not ground truth.

  • The label (impressions declining >20%) is a proxy for "at risk," not a direct measure of business harm; some declines are seasonal or expected.
  • The baseline comparison is not perfectly apples-to-apples with the original rule because CTR, engagement rate, and content age were unavailable in this feature table.
  • Prior impression volume dominates feature importance, and it is also mechanically related to how the label itself is computed. This relationship deserves further scrutiny rather than being interpreted as a causal signal.
  • Results are observed associations in this dataset and time window. They are not evidence of what refreshing any individual page will do to its traffic.

6. Ranked Recommendations

Pages flagged "declining" by the model, ranked by predicted probability, feed a queue with reason codes carried over from the earlier rule-based framework (LOW_CTR, LOW_POSITION, STALE_CONTENT) where available. When the model flags risk but the rule-based reason codes do not apply cleanly, the queue defaults to Review.

  • High-confidence decline + low query diversity: improve SEO coverage and broaden query targeting.
  • High-confidence decline + high rare-query share: review whether the content is too niche or thin and consider expanding useful coverage.
  • Borderline predictions: conduct manual review before taking action rather than automatically refreshing the page.
